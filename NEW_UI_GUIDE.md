## 📊 Modal Content Examples

### Step 1 Modal:
```
📤 Step 1: Seller Files GSTR-1

What Happens:
The seller (supplier) files their GSTR-1 return with the government, 
declaring all outward supplies (sales) made during the month.

Information Included:
• Invoice numbers and dates
• Buyer GSTIN
• Invoice amounts
• GST charged (CGST, SGST, IGST)

Why It Matters:
This is the source of truth. If a seller doesn't file GSTR-1 or files 
incorrect information, the buyer cannot claim ITC.
```

### Step 2 Modal:
```
🏛️ Step 2: Government Generates GSTR-2B

What Happens:
The government automatically generates GSTR-2B for each buyer based on 
the GSTR-1 filed by all their suppliers.

Key Point:
The buyer CANNOT modify GSTR-2B. It is a read-only document generated 
by the government system.

Why It Matters:
GSTR-2B is the government's official record of what ITC the buyer is 
eligible to claim. Any claim beyond this is invalid.
```

### Step 3 Modal:
```
💼 Step 3: Buyer Claims ITC in GSTR-3B

The Problem:
Some buyers claim MORE ITC than what appears in their GSTR-2B, either 
by mistake or intentionally.

Example:
• GSTR-2B shows: ₹1,800 ITC eligible
• Buyer claims in GSTR-3B: ₹2,200
• Difference: ₹400 (INVALID CLAIM)

Why It Matters:
Invalid ITC claims lead to penalties, interest, and potential fraud 
charges. Our system catches these mismatches.
```
